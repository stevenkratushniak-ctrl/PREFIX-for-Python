# CONTROLLED RELEASE STRATEGY

## Release Posture

`PREFIX for Python` should launch as a controlled engineering release, not as a mass-market software drop.

The product signal should be:

- careful
- selective
- trust-first
- infrastructure-grade

## Recommended Public Framing

Preferred framing:

- Founding Operator Cohort
- Controlled Evaluation Release
- Infrastructure Preview Program
- Deterministic Enforcement Pilot

Avoid:

- open beta
- viral launch
- AI experiment
- free-for-all early access

## Rollout Phases

### Phase 1: Named Invitations

- identify a small list of qualified engineers and teams
- invite directly with a clear operator brief
- frame access as a curated evaluation, not a growth loop

### Phase 2: Controlled Evaluation

- 30-day evaluation window
- weekly check-ins
- structured support and feedback capture
- explicit success criteria

### Phase 3: Conversion Review

- day 21 conversion conversation for interested operators
- day 30 decision on paid continuation
- route larger organizations into enterprise follow-up

## Suggested Cohort Size

- initial target: `8-15` teams
- target individual operator count: `20-60`

## Why Controlled Access Matters

The product is building a new category.

That means the earliest users must experience:

- boundedness
- seriousness
- trust
- direct support

Broad uncontrolled distribution would work against the product’s strongest quality signals.

## Scarcity Tone

The right tone is not hype.

The right tone is quiet confidence:

we are scaling carefully because operator trust matters more than fast volume.
