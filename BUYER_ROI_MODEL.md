# BUYER ROI MODEL

## Baseline ROI Logic

The pricing question is not whether `PREFIX` is cheaper than a plugin.

The pricing question is whether `PREFIX` costs less than the engineering waste it removes.

## Example 1: Individual Professional

Assume:

- 10 minutes per day saved from avoided invalid-state debugging and cleanup
- 20 work days per month
- 200 minutes saved per month
- roughly 3.3 hours saved per month

At a fully loaded engineering cost of `$100/hour`, that is about `$330/month` of protected time.

At a Pro price between `$19` and `$39/month`, the spend is a small fraction of recovered value.

## Example 2: Five-Person Team

Assume:

- 5 developers
- 15 minutes per developer per day saved
- 20 work days per month
- 25 hours saved per month across the team

At `$100/hour`, that is `$2,500/month` in recovered time before defect-prevention value is counted.

At `$49-$99/user/month`, Team pricing stays well below the recovered time value.

## Example 3: Small Enterprise Group

Assume:

- 20 developers
- one avoided production-adjacent defect investigation per quarter
- reduced review churn and onboarding overhead
- stronger trust in automated correction adoption

Even a modest reduction in disruption can outweigh a `$10k-$50k/year` annual contract.

## Non-Time ROI

ROI is not just time:

- fewer broken commits
- fewer invalid edits entering review
- safer mutation
- clearer auditability
- lower organizational resistance to automation

## Buyer Conclusion

If `PREFIX` saves even small amounts of engineering time while reducing mutation risk, premium pricing is economically rational.
